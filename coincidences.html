Online coincidences

The Coincidences page (sidebar → Spectra → Coincidences) builds spectra of channels gated by other channels, live, while the run is going. It is a view on the data: the run files keep every hit, so a gate set up wrong costs nothing — fix it and the gate starts over.

1. A gate is a sentence

Each gate reads as one sentence, filled in on its card:

Spectrum of [channels] [in coincidence with | with a veto from] [partner channels] within ± [window] ns (at least n of them)

  • In coincidence with keeps a hit of the channels when at least one partner (or n different partners, when there are several) fires within the window around it, before or after. Example: the HPGe spectrum in coincidence with a scintillator.

  • With a veto from keeps a hit when no partner fires within the window — a Compton or cosmic-ray veto. The vetoed spectrum and the coincidence spectrum of the same gate add up to the singles spectrum.

Click Channels or Partners to pick them from a grid of every board’s channels. Then Save: the gate is built from the next hit on (or from the next run).

Optional:

  • Energy–energy matrix: the energy of the gated channel against the energy of its partner, for coincidence gates.

  • Δt plot range: how far either side the Δt spectrum reaches (below).

  • Sum spectrum: the energy of a hit and of its partners added, in keV, for coincidence gates (section 3).

2. What a gate shows

  • Counts and rates: how many hits of the channels passed, the fraction of all their hits, and the rate over the last few seconds against the singles rate.

  • The gated (or vetoed) spectrum of each channel of the gate, with the same energy calibration and rebinning as on the Histograms page.

  • The Δt spectrum, t(partner) − t(channel), for all partners together or one by one. Real coincidences make a peak; the flat floor under it is chance. Set the window so it covers the peak, and not much more.

  • The matrix, if switched on.

  • The sum spectrum, if switched on, with the number of sums and how many fell above its range.

3. Sum spectrum

For a coincidence gate, Sum spectrum adds up, for every hit of the gate’s channels that passes the gate, its calibrated energy and that of its partners within the window, and fills one spectrum per gate with the total (keV). A cascade whose gammas land in different detectors shows up as a peak at the energy of the level it started from.

  • Partners: all in the window adds every partner hit within ± window, on any partner channel. Nearest of each channel adds only the hit of each partner channel nearest in time, which keeps a second, unrelated hit on the same detector out of the sum.

  • The fold is the gate’s own condition: a hit is summed when at least n partner channels fired (at least n of them), the same hits that go into the gated spectrum.

  • Each event is summed once. When the channels and the partners are different detectors, the sum is built from each gated hit to its partners. When a detector is on both sides (channels A, B in coincidence with A, B), the event A+B would otherwise be summed twice, once from A and once from B. It is summed only from its earliest hit, the anchor: a hit is skipped when an earlier hit within the window, on a channel that is both gated and a partner, would have summed it. Hits at exactly the same time are ordered the same way every time. In a long chain of hits each closer than the window to the next, the later ones are skipped too; at the rates where that happens the sum is dominated by chance anyway.

  • Every channel of the gate needs an energy calibration, on both sides, since energies from different detectors can only be added in keV. Until they have one, the gate’s card says which channels lack it and the sum stays empty; the rest of the gate works. Calibrate them from their cards on the Histograms page. The calibrations are read when the gate starts and checked every second: a new calibration of one of the gate’s channels during the run empties the sum spectrum and starts it again (the card says when), because its bins would otherwise mix two energy scales.

  • Range and bin width (default 0 – 16384 keV in 1 keV bins). Sums above the range are counted as Above … keV on the card, never dropped silently; sums below 0 keV (a negative calibration offset) are counted too.

Chance coincidences under the sum peak. A partner hit that fired by chance within the window adds its energy to the sum like a real one and moves the event out of its peak, or a chance pair makes a sum where there is no real event. Judge how many there are from the Δt plot: the flat floor is chance, so the floor level per ns × 2 × window, compared with the counts in the peak, is the fraction of summed events that are chance. Narrow the window to the Δt peak (after Align in time) to keep it small; nearest helps when one detector fires often.

4. Aligning channels in time

Cables, detectors and shaping put each channel’s signal at its own delay, so the Δt peak of a real coincidence is rarely at 0. Once a gate has collected a peak, Align in time shifts each partner’s time offset so its peak sits at 0 against the gate’s first channel. The gate then starts over with the new offsets, and a narrow window is enough.

The offsets are listed, and can be edited, under Timing settings. They are per channel and shared by every gate.

5. Only channels on one clock

Two hits can only be compared if they were timed by the same clock. That is the case for the channels of one board, and for the CAEN boards of a synchronised chain (see the CAEN settings) that also share one clock: every board but the first takes its clock from CLK-IN (Acquisition Control 0x8100, bit 6). Boards started together but each on its own oscillator are not on one clock. Boards that run on their own clocks — two independent CAEN boards, or a CAEN board and an SIS3316 — drift apart by microseconds within seconds, so no gate can mix them. The channel picker greys out the boards that do not share a clock with what is already picked, and a saved gate that mixes them says so on its card.

6. Time between hits

At the bottom of the page, pick any channel to see the time between its consecutive hits, on a log time axis from 1 ns to 10 s. A random source gives one smooth bump at 1/rate. Look for:

  • a cut on the left of the bump: the dead time;

  • a spike at short times: double triggering, or ringing;

  • sharp lines: a pulser, or periodic noise.

7. How it works, and what it costs

The boards involved in a gate export their hits (16 bytes each; boards not in any gate export nothing). Every quarter of a second the server collects them, sorts them in time per clock and evaluates the gates.

A board does not deliver its hits in time order: a channel’s events wait in the board until its buffer is read out, and a quiet channel waits longer. So a hit is only evaluated once every active board of its clock has delivered data at least Wait for slower channels later (Timing settings, default 2 s of data time). A hit arriving after its time has already been evaluated is counted as late in the page header and left out of the gates (it is still in the singles spectra and the file). If late hits appear, raise that latency; it costs only display delay and a little memory.

Memory stays bounded: the pending hits are shown in the header in MB, and are capped (about 64 MB per clock) whatever the rate. Each gate’s outputs are fixed-size arrays, made when the gate starts and never grown: 4 bytes per bin of each gated channel’s spectrum (128 kB for a 32768-bin channel), 4 kB per Δt spectrum (one per channel–partner pair), 1 MB for the energy–energy matrix when it is on (512 × 512), and for the sum spectrum 4 bytes per bin, 64 kB at the default 16384 bins and at most 256 kB (65536 bins). With the most of everything (16 gates of 32 + 32 channels) that is about 200 MB; a usual setup of a few gates over a few channels stays within a few MB. Hits the online decoder had to skip because it was behind are shown as not seen: the coincidence rates miss them, the file does not.

The configuration is stored in conf/coincidences.json in the working directory, like the other dashboards, so it is the same for everyone and survives a restart.
